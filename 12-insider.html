The Careless Hand.

Most data leaks don't involve a hacker. They involve a tired person, an autocomplete, a "quick" upload to a personal drive, or a spreadsheet pasted into a public AI chatbot.

Runtime · 8 minutes · Interactive
Human error
— misdelivery, misconfiguration, oversharing — is behind a large share of reported breaches
1 field
— the "To:" line — is the most common way sensitive data leaves a company
AI tools
are a new leak path when confidential data is pasted into unapproved services

"Insider threat" sounds like spies. Occasionally it is — a disgruntled employee taking a client list. But far more often it's a good employee in a hurry. This module is about the small habits that keep company and customer data where it belongs, and spotting the rare warning signs of something worse.

Act 03 · Where data belongs

Know what you're holding.

Most organisations label data by sensitivity. Your company's names may differ, but the idea is the same:

PUBLICWebsite content, press releases, published rates.
INTERNALOrg charts, internal announcements, procedures.
CONFIDENTIALContracts, financial reports, strategy, HR data.
RESTRICTEDCustomer/member PII, SSNs, account numbers, credentials.

OK or not OK?

Pasting a customer complaint (with name and account number) into a free public AI chatbot to draft a reply
Public AI tools may store and train on what you paste. Use only company-approved AI tools, and remove personal details unless the tool is approved for them.
Uploading a confidential report to your personal Google Drive to work on it at the weekend
Company data leaves company control (and monitoring). Use approved remote access or company cloud storage instead.
Sharing a confidential file with a named colleague via the company's SharePoint/OneDrive link set to "specific people"
Right tool, right audience, access controlled. Avoid "anyone with the link" for anything beyond public data.
Leaving printed member statements on the shared printer while you grab coffee
Collect printouts immediately (use secure "pull" printing if available) and shred restricted paper — never the regular bin.
Forwarding work emails to your personal account "for reference" before you change jobs
This is data exfiltration — even with good intentions — and may breach your agreement and the law. Ask your manager what you may keep.
Posting a published press release on your LinkedIn
Public data is meant to be shared. Just don't add internal context that isn't public yet.

Act 04 · Your playbook

Handle data like it's someone's life.

RULE 01
Check the To: line.
Pause before send on anything sensitive. Autocomplete is fast — and wrong surprisingly often.
RULE 02
Share the minimum.
Only the fields, people, and time period the task requires. Strip what isn't needed.
RULE 03
Approved tools only.
Company storage, secure file-share, encrypted email, approved AI tools. No personal drives or inboxes.
RULE 04
Paper counts.
Collect printouts, lock away documents, shred restricted paper.
RULE 05
Mistakes: report fast.
Misdirected email, lost device, wrong file shared — tell Security immediately. Speed limits the damage.
RULE 06
Concerns: report quietly.
Suspicious behaviour goes to Security or HR confidentially. Don't confront or investigate yourself.
The firewall can't stop you from attaching the wrong file. Only a pause can.
