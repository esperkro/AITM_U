"Could you grab the door? My hands are full." The most expensive badge reader in the world loses to good manners every single day.

Social engineering is manipulating people instead of machines. In person it looks like tailgating (following you through a secure door), pretexting (posing as a contractor, auditor, or new hire), and plain old charm. You don't need to be suspicious of everyone — you need a few polite scripts that work every time.

Act 03 · The levers they pull

Six buttons on every human.

Social engineers use the same handful of psychological levers. Once you can name them, you'll feel them being pulled.

HelpfulnessFull hands, a lost badge, a stressed newcomer. You want to help — they want you to.
AuthorityUniforms, titles, "the director asked me to…". We defer without checking.
Urgency"I'm late", "it'll go down in an hour". Rushing skips verification.
FamiliarityCompany swag, insider names, small talk. Feels like one of us.
Avoiding awkwardnessChallenging someone feels rude. They count on that discomfort.
ReciprocityA coffee, a compliment, a favour first — then the ask.

Act 04 · Your playbook

Polite. Firm. Every time.

RULE 01
One badge, one person.
Never let someone through a secure door on your badge — not couriers, not coworkers, not new hires.
RULE 02
Wear your badge, expect others to.
Visible badges make strangers stand out. Offer help to anyone without one.
RULE 03
Verify with a call.
Contractors, auditors, inspectors: confirm through the directory number before granting access or sharing information.
RULE 04
Visitors get escorts.
Visitors sign in at reception and are accompanied at all times — especially near network rooms and finance.
RULE 05
Clean desk, locked screen.
An intruder's best loot is what's left out: printouts, sticky-note passwords, unlocked laptops.
RULE 06
Report the odd ones.
Someone pushy or out of place? Tell Security — even if it turned out fine. Patterns matter.
Holding the door is kind. Checking who's walking through it is kinder — to everyone inside.
