The First Nine Minutes.

You clicked. Or you think you did. Or something just felt off. What happens next depends almost entirely on how quickly you say something.

Runtime · 5 minutes · Interactive

Security teams don't need you to be certain. They need you to be fast. Every module in this suite ends the same way — "report it" — so this one shows why speed matters, what to include, and why embarrassment is never a reason to wait.

Minutes
Reported quickly: password reset, sessions revoked, email pulled from other inboxes. Often contained before any damage.
Hours
Attacker reads mail, sets forwarding rules, phishes coworkers from your account.
Days
Data stolen, fraudulent payments sent, ransomware staged across the network.
You will not get in trouble for reporting. A report that turns out to be nothing costs Security a few minutes. A mistake that goes unreported can cost the company millions. Good security teams thank reporters — including people who clicked.

Act 02 · Report or not?

When in doubt, report.

Decide: does each situation deserve a report?

You entered your password on a page, then realised the URL looked wrong
Report immediately — this is the single most time-critical case. Then change the password from a trusted device if Security tells you to.
A phishing email you recognised and didn't click
Still report (use the Report Phishing button). It lets Security remove it from everyone else's inbox — the coworker who would have clicked.
An MFA prompt you didn't trigger — and you denied it
A prompt means someone has your password. Report so Security can block them and help you reset.
You opened an attachment and "nothing happened"
Malware is designed to look like nothing happened. Report so your device can be checked.
You emailed a spreadsheet to the wrong person
Data incidents count too. Privacy teams may have legal deadlines measured in hours or days.
A newsletter you signed up for arrives as normal
Expected and harmless. If it's annoying, unsubscribe — that's not a security issue.

Act 04 · Your playbook

Speed over certainty.

RULE 01
Report first, worry later.
You don't need proof. "Something felt off" is a valid report.
RULE 02
Know your channels.
Report Phishing button for emails; the security hotline or service desk for anything urgent. Save the number in your phone today.
RULE 03
Urgent = call.
Entered a password, approved a prompt, ran a file, sent data to the wrong place? Phone, don't ticket.
RULE 04
Include the facts.
What, when, what you did, which device/account, the original message, anything odd since.
RULE 05
Never send passwords.
Security will never ask for your password — not even to investigate.
RULE 06
Preserve, don't clean.
Keep the evidence. Follow Security's instructions on disconnecting or resetting.
Embarrassment lasts a minute. A breach lasts a year. Report it.
