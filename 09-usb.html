The Gift Nobody Sent.

A labeled USB stick by your car. A free charging cable at a conference. A "replacement keyboard" that showed up in the mail. Every one of them can be a computer pretending to be something harmless.

Hardware attacks exploit the oldest instinct there is: curiosity, and the urge to return lost property. The device doesn't need you to open a file. Some announce themselves as a keyboard and type commands faster than you can blink. Others just need a few seconds of power.

Act 03 · Plug it in, or not?

Hardware you didn't buy.

Sort each device: fine to connect, or don't connect?

Branded USB stick in a "thank you for your business" envelope
Mailed to your desk; you don't recognize the sender
Unsolicited hardware by mail is a known attack. Hand it to IT.
"Free" charging cable from a stall at a conference
Looks like a normal USB-C cable
Malicious cables exist that look identical to normal ones and contain hidden electronics. Use your own cable and charger.
Public USB charging port at an airport
Your phone is at 4%
Prefer a wall outlet with your own charger, or carry a power bank. If you must use the port, a "data blocker" adapter (charge-only) helps. Never tap "Trust this computer" on your phone.
Headset issued by IT through the normal equipment request
Asset tag, delivered by IT
Known source, normal process. This is what trustworthy hardware looks like.
Visitor asks to plug their USB drive into your PC to print their presentation
Friendly, in a hurry, meeting starts in 5 minutes
Politely decline and point them to reception or IT — or ask them to email it to the organizer. Time pressure is part of the pitch.

Could be a keylogger. Don't unplug it yourself — report it to Security so they can investigate and preserve evidence.

Act 04 · Your playbook

If you didn't buy it, don't plug it.

RULE 01
Found it? Hand it in.
Never plug in found drives — at work or at home. Give them to IT/Security with where and when you found them.
RULE 02
Only IT-issued hardware.
Keyboards, mice, drives, docks, and cables for work devices should come through IT.
RULE 03
Charge with your own gear.
Your charger, your cable, a wall socket or power bank. Never "trust" an unknown computer from your phone.
RULE 04
Use approved file sharing.
Need to move files? Use company cloud storage or email — not a stick passed around.
RULE 05
Spot something odd? Report.
An unfamiliar device attached to a PC, network port, or printer should be reported, not removed.
The best attacks feel like a favour — returning a lost drive, charging a dead phone. Kindness is fine. Plugging in is not.
