Endless Knocking.

They already have your password. The only thing between them and your account is one tap on your phone — so they'll keep asking until you give it to them.

Runtime · 6 minutes · Interactive

Real breaches
at major tech companies began with exactly this: repeated prompts plus a fake "IT" message

Multi-factor authentication is one of the best protections you have. But push-style MFA has a human weak spot: it asks you to approve something, and humans eventually say yes just to make the noise stop. This is called MFA fatigue, or push bombing.

Every denial keeps them out. But notice what it means: someone has your password right now. The next steps matter just as much — report it and change your password. Continue to see the full playbook.

Act 03 · The trap

Unexpected prompt. Now what?

You receive an approval request you didn't trigger. Then four more. Choose your response:

Number matching: your built-in lie detector

Many organizations now use number matching: the sign-in screen shows a number and your phone asks you to type it. If you didn't start a sign-in, you have no screen and no number — that's your proof the request isn't yours.

_ _
"Enter the number shown to sign in"
If a "helpful" caller or chat message ever tells you which number to enter, that is the attacker reading it off their screen. Deny and report.
Act 04 · Your playbook

Rules for every buzz.

RULE 01
Didn't start it? Deny it.
Approve only prompts you triggered seconds ago, on a sign-in you're looking at. Everything else is a deny — no exceptions.
RULE 02
A prompt means a leaked password.
Prompts only appear after a correct password. Change it from a trusted device, and anywhere else you reused it.
RULE 03
IT never asks you to approve.
Not by phone, chat, text, or email. A message telling you to accept a prompt is part of the attack.
RULE 04
Never read out or type a number for someone.
Number-matching codes and one-time codes belong to you alone. Anyone asking for one is trying to sign in as you.
RULE 05
Report — even if you denied.
Your report lets Security block the attacker, reset sessions, and catch the same campaign hitting coworkers.
The attacker is counting on you being tired. Be stubborn instead.
