The Handed Key.

No fake login page. No stolen password. You sign in to the real Microsoft or Google page — and then you hand the attacker a key yourself, by clicking "Accept".

Runtime · 8 minutes · Interactive
0
passwords stolen — the attacker never needs yours
MFA
doesn't help: you completed it yourself before granting access
Persists
even after you change your password — until the app is removed

"Sign in with Microsoft" and "Sign in with Google" let apps work with your data through OAuth — a system of permission slips. Normally that's great. But an attacker can register their own app, give it a friendly name, and ask you for a permission slip to read your mail, your files, and act as you — indefinitely.

Act 03 · Permissions decoded

What the words actually mean.

Consent screen saysWhat the app can actually do
Sign you in and read your profileSee your name, email, and photo. Low risk — this is what most "sign in with" apps need.
Read your mailSearch years of email: invoices, password resets, contracts, HR messages.
Send mail as youPhish your coworkers and customers from your real account. Classic business email compromise.
Access all files you can accessDownload everything in OneDrive, SharePoint, and Teams you have rights to — not just your own.
Maintain access to data…Keep all of the above working long after you've closed the tab. Password resets don't revoke it.

Would you grant it? Sort each request.

Company-approved scheduling tool asks to "Read your calendar"
Listed in your company's app catalog · verified publisher
Matches the task, approved by IT, verified publisher. That's how normal consent looks.
"PDF Converter Pro" asks to "Read and write all your mail"
Linked from an email · unverified
A converter needs one file, not your mailbox. Permissions far bigger than the job = refuse.
"HR Benefits Portal 2026" asks for "Files.ReadWrite.All" and offline access
Email says you must enroll by Friday
Urgency + broad file access + persistent access. Check with HR via a channel you already know before granting anything.
Browser game asks to "Sign you in" and "Read your contacts and mail"
Using your work account
Never connect personal or entertainment apps to your work identity — and certainly not with mail access.
Already clicked Accept? Report it immediately. Security can revoke the app's tokens. You can also review apps you've granted at your account's "My Apps" / "Third-party access" page (Microsoft: myapps.microsoft.com · Google: myaccount.google.com → Security → third-party connections). Changing your password alone does not remove the app.
Act 04 · Your playbook

Before you click Accept.

RULE 01
Real page ≠ safe app.
A genuine Microsoft or Google screen can still be asking you to authorize an attacker's app. Read who is asking, not who is hosting.
RULE 02
Permissions must fit the task.
Viewing a document never requires your mail, all your files, or ongoing access. Mismatch = refuse.
RULE 03
Unverified + unexpected = no.
If you didn't go looking for this app, and the publisher isn't verified or approved by IT, cancel.
RULE 04
Use the company app catalog.
Need a tool? Request it through IT. Approved apps are vetted; random links are not.
RULE 05
Granted by mistake? Report fast.
Only revoking the app stops it — a password change won't. Tell Security what you approved and when.
The most dangerous key is the one you hand over yourself — politely, on a real login page.
